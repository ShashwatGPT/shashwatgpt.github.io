Teaching the Fundamentals: My Journey as a TA for ESC101A & ESC102A

During my undergraduate years at IIT Kanpur, I had the privilege of serving as a Teaching Assistant for two of the university's foundational computer science courses: ESC101A (Fundamentals of Computing) and ESC102A (Advanced Programming). Working under the supervision of Dr. Priyanka Bagade, this experience was both a technical challenge and a profound lesson in empathy and communication.

The Challenge of First Impressions: Unlearning to Teach

When I first stepped into the ESC101A lab, I thought my job was simply to debug C code. ESC101A is the primary gateway to programming for entering freshmen at IIT Kanpur. It covers foundational concepts—from basic loops to pointers and memory management. However, I quickly realized that knowing the syntax is entirely different from teaching the logic.

I vividly remember a lab session where a student was staring blankly at a "segmentation fault." To me, having programmed for years, the error was glaring: an uninitialized pointer. But to a freshman encountering a compiler for the first time, it was a terrifying red wall of text. Trying to explain memory addresses in abstract terms only confused him more. That day, I learned to pause. I drew a row of mailboxes on a piece of paper, explaining pointers not as hexadecimal addresses, but as street numbers pointing to specific houses. Seeing the "aha!" moment light up his face was incredibly fulfilling. My biggest challenge became my greatest goal: to demystify programming by unlearning my own intuition and stepping into the shoes of a beginner.

Escaping the Echo Chamber: Bridging the Language Gap

As the semester progressed, another dimension of teaching presented itself. During a late-evening office hour, a student approached me, clearly frustrated. He was diligent and attended every lecture, yet he struggled to translate the logic to code. As we talked, I realized the core issue wasn't mathematical aptitude—it was the medium of instruction. The technical jargon, delivered entirely in rapid English during lectures, was an invisible barrier.

Recognizing this, I pivoted my approach for my lab group. I started holding dedicated debugging sessions where I explained concepts like recursion and dynamic memory allocation in Hindi. We stripped away the imposing English terminology and talked about logic in our native tongue. Giving that student the space to ask "yeh function wapas call kyu ho raha hai?" (why is this function calling itself again?) without hesitation transformed his trajectory in the course. This experience was a powerful reminder that inclusive teaching means adjusting your frequency to match your students', ensuring no one is left behind due to arbitrary barriers.

Scaling Up to ESC102A: From Correctness to Design

The transition to serving as a TA for ESC102A tested my growth as an educator. This advanced course delves into sophisticated data structures and algorithmic complexity. Working closely with Dr. Priyanka Bagade, our focus shifted from simply making the code compile to fostering robust software engineering principles.

Under Dr. Bagade's guidance, I took an active role in crafting practical assignments. Instead of standard textbook algorithms, I designed problem statements that mimicked real-world scenarios—challenging students to not only find a solution but to evaluate its efficiency and elegance. During evaluations, my feedback evolved. I wasn't just checking for correct outputs anymore; I was sitting down with students to discuss edge cases, code refactoring, and why an O(n^2) approach might fail at scale. I was no longer just debugging their code; I was debugging their thought process.

Reflections on Mentorship

Looking back, my time as a TA was one of the defining chapters of my undergraduate journey. It transformed my own technical foundations, forcing me to understand concepts deeply enough to explain them simply. More importantly, it taught me that leadership in technology isn't just about writing the best code—it's about lifting others up, breaking down complex barriers, and empowering the next generation of engineers to find their footing.
